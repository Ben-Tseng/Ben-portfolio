Hi, I’m Bin Zeng (Ben).

I am pursuing an M.S. in Computer Science at Georgia Tech (OMSCS), specializing in Machine Learning. My work centers on AI decision-making under uncertainty, trustworthy agentic systems, and multi-agent collaboration.

My background spans industrial engineering, technology entrepreneurship, venture investment, and investigation workflows. These experiences motivate my interest in how intelligent systems combine incomplete evidence, reason about risk, and make decisions that people can understand and scrutinize.

Research interests

Systems that reason, collaborate, and remain accountable.

I am interested in intelligent systems whose decisions remain inspectable when evidence is incomplete, environments change, or other agents cannot be fully trusted.

Background

Before returning to formal computer science study, I worked in venture capital and technology investment and founded a decision-support software platform. My professional experience also includes seller investigation and verification at Amazon.

Research direction

I am preparing for Fall 2027 PhD applications, seeking to develop reliable AI systems that reason and act under uncertainty, collaborate effectively, and remain robust when information or other agents cannot be trusted.

GPU-Accelerated Inventory Optimization

An independent reproduction and critical analysis of a published approach to perishable-inventory control using GPU-accelerated value iteration. The project examines implementation choices, computational performance, and reproducibility.

This is not a claim of an original inventory-control algorithm.

Exploratory engineering

Financial Decision Support

AlphaShort & MacroSignal

AlphaShort combines price positioning, momentum, crowding risk, and volatility regimes with configurable historical backtesting. MacroSignal organizes FRED indicators for monitoring U.S. inflation risk.

These tools are exploratory engineering projects, not evidence of validated forecasting performance.
